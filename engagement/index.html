Leadership roles

At Manchester I am Associate Dean for Public, Civic and Schools Engagement in the Faculty of Science and Engineering, providing strategic leadership for engagement across the Faculty.

From 2022 to 2024 I served as Coordinator of the Education and Outreach group of the ATLAS Collaboration — an elected position, on the votes of 181 collaborating institutes, with strategic oversight of all of ATLAS's educational and outreach activity. The role hosts more than 55,000 visitors a year to the ATLAS Visitor Centre and reaches millions online, coordinating content across atlas.cern, social media, educational materials, and the flagship Open Data initiative at opendata.atlas.cern; reporting directly to the ATLAS spokesperson.

I also represent (2017–2024) the UK on the Collaboration Board of the International Particle Physics Outreach Group (IPPOG).

Activities

  • Official CERN guide for the ATLAS Control Room and Visitor Centre, the Antimatter Factory, and the Synchrocyclotron.
  • Organiser of the annual Manchester Schools Particle Physics Masterclass, reaching thousands of A-level students, expanding to widening-participation audiences.
  • Registered STEM Ambassador; exhibitor at the Manchester Science Festival, the Big Bang Fair, and Science Uncovered (European Researchers' Night).
  • “Understanding the Higgs boson” at the Royal Society Summer Science Exhibition.
  • Founder and curator of the Manchester particle group's social presence (~300k views/year).
  • Author of two complementary interactive dark-matter explorers — browser-based pedagogical tools. The sensitivity explorer lets readers build their own experiment, dialling in target, exposure, energy thresholds, background and detector depth, and watch their 90% C.L. sensitivity curve move live against DarkSide-50, LZ and DarkSide-20k. The recoil explorer takes the other end of the problem — given a hypothetical dark-matter particle, what actually arrives? The flux through your hand, the halo velocity distribution, the nuclear-recoil spectrum a detector records, and a Poisson “run the experiment” toy that turns expected counts into real, fluctuating data so a single event never gets confused with a discovery.
