Public engagement & outreach

I am currently leading the organisation of the British Science Festival 2027 as Chair of the Manchester Leadership Group — one of the UK's largest public science festivals. Alongside this I sustain a broad, ongoing programme of public, schools and civic engagement:

  • Official CERN guide for the public — hosting VIP visits and meeting MPs, AMs, diplomats and heads of state.
  • Organiser of the annual Manchester Schools Particle Physics Masterclass, reaching hundreds of A-level students a year and expanding to widening-participation audiences.
  • Registered STEM Ambassador; exhibitor and demonstrator at the Manchester Science Festival, the Big Bang Fair, Science Uncovered (European Researchers' Night) and the Royal Society Summer Science Exhibition.
  • Invited lecturer at teacher-development sessions in the UK and abroad — including the Institute for Research in Schools and courses at CERN — and a frequent visitor for school talks and careers Q&As.

Interactive tools

I build browser-based pedagogical tools that let anyone explore the physics of dark-matter detection directly — no installation, no maths prerequisites.

  • The sensitivity explorer — "How do we hunt for dark matter?" Build your own experiment by dialling in target, exposure, energy thresholds, background and detector depth, and watch your 90% C.L. sensitivity curve move live against published results from CRESST-III, DarkSide-50, PandaX-4T, LZ and DarkSide-20k, and against the neutrino fog.
  • The recoil explorer — "What does dark matter look like in a detector?" Given a hypothetical dark-matter particle, see the flux through your hand, the halo velocity distribution, the nuclear-recoil spectrum a detector records, and a Poisson “run the experiment” toy that turns expected counts into real, fluctuating data — so a single event never gets confused with a discovery.
