I am an experimental particle physicist. Over the past two decades I have worked as part of the ATLAS, DØ and DarkSide collaborations, and led the team that discovered the first new particle observed at the Large Hadron Collider. My work moves between the very large and the very quiet: searching for new physics in high-energy collisions at CERN, and listening for the rare whispers of dark matter in deep-underground detectors.

My route here ran through the Mathematical Tripos at Cambridge, a Master's at Manchester, a PhD at Lancaster, and research positions based at Fermilab near Chicago and at CERN in Geneva, before fellowships — Marie Curie, Ernest Rutherford, a University Presidential Fellowship, and a Turing Fellowship — brought me back to Manchester, where I am now a Professor.

What I work on

I deliberately work across several research communities rather than within a single one. The connecting thread is using the best available data, and the methods to interpret it, to ask what lies beyond the Standard Model:

  • Dark matter — both at the LHC and through the DarkSide argon direct-detection programme, which I established at Manchester.
  • Electroweak physics, QCD and B-physics at hadron colliders.
  • Neutrinos — heavy Majorana and sterile searches, and coherent neutrino scattering with argon.
  • Instrumentation and emerging levitating-sensor technology.
  • Data-intensive science & AI — machine-learning tools for scientific discovery, as a Turing Fellow.

Beyond research, I direct a national Centre for Doctoral Training, advise UK research funders, coordinate education and outreach for the ATLAS experiment at CERN, and lead public, civic and schools engagement across Manchester's Faculty of Science and Engineering.
